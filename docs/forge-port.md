# Forge 1.20.1 port verification

The reference is NuclearCraft: Overhauled 2o.9.7 at `ef28c65`. The port builds with Java 17, Mojang mappings, and Forge 47.4.10, matching the Forge build installed in Auram. The Forge code currently comes from the legacy NuclearCraft source and uses no Neohaul implementation code.

## Gameplay coverage

- The Melter has an in-world block, menu, datapack recipes for ice, packed ice, and sulfur dust, and a Forge fluid registration for molten sulfur.
- The headless GameTest runs the full ice recipe, asserts its 4,000 FE cost and 1,000 mB water output, simulates and executes capability transfers, checks removal invalidation, and reloads the saved output tank.
- The reobfuscated development JAR booted in a clean Forge 47.4.10 dedicated server and produced 1,000 mB of water from ice in-world. The server stopped cleanly.
- The first solid-fuel reactor uses the original cuboidal frame and wall rules, with outward-only Forge item and fluid ports. It has a controller menu and can run on a redstone signal. The first supported combination is LECf-251 oxide fuel, a heavy water moderator, a beryllium-carbon reflector, and water heat sinks.
- The headless reactor tests assembled a five-block-wide shell, inserted one fuel and 1,000 mB of water through the ports, and verified one depleted fuel item and 4,000 mB of high-pressure steam. A separate test broke and repaired the shell, checked invalidation of previously acquired handlers, and loaded the saved coolant into a replacement controller.
- The steam turbine validates opposed inlets and outlets, centered bearings, a full shaft, matching blades per stage, and dynamo coil placement. It has a controller menu, redstone activation, three blade tiers, a stator, six coil tiers, and coil connectors. It accepts steam through an outward-facing Forge fluid handler and exposes exhaust and energy on the corresponding outward faces. It can also push into neighboring tanks and energy storage.
- Four headless turbine tests cover sided access, simulated transfers, full exhaust backpressure, save and reassembly, reversed flow through a two-wide shaft, rejection of mixed blade tiers, and a complete reactor-to-turbine-to-Melter chain.
- The legacy ore, ingot, dust, storage block, alloy, gem, compound, and part families have individual registry IDs. The port includes the original crafting patterns for the Melter and available solid-fission and turbine parts, plus vanilla smelting and blasting for ores and metal dusts. A headless GameTest crafted copper wire with mixed vanilla and NuclearCraft ingots and verified that crafting a water heat sink returns the bucket.
- A new world on a clean Forge dedicated server generated all eight legacy NuclearCraft ores using the packaged JAR. The ore recipes and drops preserve the default legacy ore-to-ingot progression.
- The Manufactory and Alloy Furnace have legacy crafting patterns, block models and screens. They accept Forge Energy on all faces and support per-face item modes. Their datapack recipes cover the graphite chain, ore processing, bronze, steel, ferroboron, tough alloy, and other starter materials, with optional outputs for installed tech mods.
- Headless tests verified the 5,000 FE coal-to-graphite chain and the 8,000 FE bronze recipe with either ingredient order. The Alloy Furnace test checked a full output, handler invalidation, and restoration of in-progress processing and face modes. Optional JEI categories cover all currently ported processing, reactor fuel, and turbine recipes.
- The Separator uses two extractable outputs and waits for room in both slots before spending energy or ingredients. Its regular material and fuel recipes are available through Forge tags. The solid-fission catalog contains 81 oxide, nitride, and zirconium alloy recipes from the original fuel tables, with legacy times, heat, efficiency, criticality, intrinsic flux, and self-priming values.
- The headless Separator test verified a 40,000 FE batch of ten uranium dust, a stall when the second output was full, and the exact nine-to-one output balance. The recovered items supplied the vanilla crafting recipe for nine unprocessed LEU-235 pellets.
- A hidden Forge development client joined a clean dedicated server running the packaged JAR, opened the Alloy Furnace screen, and synced an input-only face change from the server. JEI 15.20.0.129 found the bronze recipe after login. The temporary test copy remapped JEI's access transformer for the development client. The client measured master volume at zero, and the native observer recorded no visible windows or focus changes.
- A second hidden-client test opened the Separator, synced a side setting, and found both uranium outputs in JEI after multiplayer recipe sync. Master volume was zero and the native observer recorded no visible windows or focus changes.
- The Infuser accepts a counted item and a measured fluid ingredient. Its 139 packaged recipes include pellet and isotope oxide and nitride conversions, metal oxides, the heavy water moderator, concrete, and recipes with optional Thermal or Immersive Engineering outputs. Oxygen, nitrogen, and heavy water are registered fluids. Each Infuser face independently supports fluid input, output, auto-output, or disabled alongside its item setting. Shift-click a face in the machine screen to cycle its fluid mode.
- Headless GameTests verified that an LEU-235 pellet uses 6,000 FE and exactly 1,000 mB of oxygen. A short fluid supply and a full output each held the batch in place. The machine restored the input fluid and face modes on reload, invalidated old fluid handlers after setting changes and removal, and retained surplus fluid when an auto-output destination filled up.
- A hidden Forge development client opened the Infuser in multiplayer, synced its 1,234 mB tank and independent face settings, and displayed the Infuser oxygen ingredient in JEI. Master volume was zero. The native observer recorded no onscreen samples or focus activations.
- The single-block Electrolyzer uses one input and four output tanks, with independent fluid settings for each face and output tank. Select a tank in the screen to configure its faces. Each output can hold fluid, void excess, or void all. Its 64 datapack recipes include water, enriched waters, chemical intermediates, and the 54 fuel and depleted-fuel fluoride recipes backed by fluids registered in the original mod. Crafting uses the original graphite, advanced plate, copper solenoid, chassis, and motor pattern.
- A headless GameTest ran an entire 3,200-tick water batch, including a full oxygen tank stall and an in-progress reload. It used 128,000 FE and 500 mB of water to produce 500 mB of hydrogen and 250 mB of oxygen in separate tanks. Other GameTests verified that oxygen auto-output feeds the Infuser and that a four-product recipe only voids excess from the configured output.
- A hidden Forge development client joined the packaged server, found all 64 Electrolyzer recipes in JEI, and verified separate tank contents and server synchronization of a selected output's face and overflow settings. Master volume was zero, and the native observer recorded no onscreen windows or focus changes.
- The Cobblestone Generator, Water Source, and Nitrogen Collector are craftable in all three legacy tiers. The basic versions make 0.125 cobblestone items, 10 mB of water, and 5 mB of nitrogen per tick. Compact and dense versions multiply those rates by 8 and 64. Each collector stores its product for extraction through Forge capabilities and can push it into adjacent inventories and tanks. A clean Forge GameTest used a compact Nitrogen Collector to supply an Infuser and make one LEU-235 nitride fuel.
- JEI displays all nine collectors. A hidden multiplayer client opened their JEI category and verified that a custom nitrogen rate supplied by the server was reflected in both the basic and compact tiers. The native observer recorded zero visible windows or focus changes, and master volume was zero.
- The Decay Hastener uses the shared Forge item and energy interfaces and has 87 recipes derived from the legacy catalog. The uranium-238 recipe takes 12,230 ticks and 122,300 FE per radium dust. A headless test produced the two dusts needed for the original shaped recipe and used them to craft two Ra-Be sources. Legacy recipes whose outputs would need thallium or barium items and additional TBP and protactinium forms still need mappings.
- Ra-Be, Po-Be, and Cf-252 neutron sources align toward the reactor interior when a solid-fission shell assembles. Redstone activates each source. The source primes the first eligible fuel cell along that line unless a full reflector blocks it. The cell still needs enough flux to run. Headless tests used a source to start LEU-235 oxide, verified reflector blocking, and applied the stronger of two sources to the same cell.
- A silent, hidden client opened the Decay Hastener, found its 87 recipes in JEI, and received a powered source's facing, activity, and custom 0.85 efficiency from the dedicated server. The focus observer recorded zero onscreen windows and zero focus changes.
- The Rock Crusher restores four recipes with three ordered output slots. Granite, diorite, and andesite accept their polished forms. Each possible output item is rolled independently. A normal andesite batch can yield up to two beryllium dust at 40% per item. The dust can be smelted into beryllium ingots. The alternate profile gives granite and andesite the original duplicated primary product slots, each at 20% per item.
- A headless test verified the default and alternate profiles against the legacy recipe values. A separate batch verified that a full third slot stalls the machine before spending energy. The same test checked guaranteed, minimum, and zero-quantity products, and verified the beryllium smelting recipe.
- A silent, hidden client opened the Rock Crusher, verified the position of all three output slots, and found the four recipes and the server-selected alternate probabilities in JEI. The native observer measured zero visible windows and focus changes.
- Solid-fission reactors accept graphite and beryllium storage blocks through the shared Forge tags. The three moderator defaults are graphite at 10 flux and 1.1× efficiency, beryllium at 22 and 1.05×, and heavy water at 36 and 1×. Moderator lines sum flux and average their efficiencies. The two reflectors use their legacy reflectivity and efficiency values. A lead-steel reflector passes a source beam, while a fully reflective block stops it.
- A headless mixed-material reactor started LEU-235 oxide and generated steam using ordinary graphite and beryllium. One line alone stayed below criticality. Adding the second line supplied 108 flux. Graphite and a reflector touching the outside of the reactor left its shell dimensions intact.
- A silent, hidden client running Auram’s JEI version listed all three moderators and both reflectors. It displayed server-configured graphite flux and lead-steel reflectivity. The native observer recorded zero visible windows or focus changes.
- The Decay Generator is crafted from lead, redstone, and cobblestone. It generates Forge Energy from adjacent thorium, uranium, and other legacy fuel blocks. The blocks change according to their original lifetime settings. It exposes an output-only energy capability and can feed adjacent machines. All seven legacy fertile-isotope storage blocks can be crafted and unpacked.
- Headless tests crafted the Decay Generator, checked energy simulation, extraction, removal, reload, and delivery to a Manufactory, and verified a custom block transformation. A silent, hidden client opened all nine generator recipes in JEI and displayed custom uranium settings from the dedicated server. Generator radiation remains part of the radiation system port.
- The Fuel Reprocessor has the original crafting pattern, eight ordered outputs, and 108 recipes covering all 27 NuclearCraft fuels in TRISO, oxide, nitride, and zirconium-alloy forms. Each batch uses nine depleted fuel items, 400 ticks, and 8,000 FE. Chance byproducts roll independently for each possible item. TRISO also returns graphite and cladding, and zirconium-alloy recipes return surplus zirconium in their original slot.
- Headless tests checked the catalog, recipe values, sided automation, and a saved batch. A full active output held the input and energy, while leftovers in the inactive output slots remained where they were. The recovered oxides matched the crafting ingredients for fresh LEP-239 fuel.
- A silent, hidden client opened the Fuel Reprocessor against a packaged dedicated server. It displayed all eight output slots, the stored energy, and 108 JEI recipes. The native observer recorded zero visible windows and focus changes.
- The Pressurizer makes pyrolytic carbon from graphite and includes the legacy gem, obsidian, sintered-material, flour, and compression recipes that have native or Forge-tagged ingredients. The Assembler coats all 27 NuclearCraft carbide fuels. Its four item inputs are shapeless and cannot reuse one input slot for two ingredients.
- Headless tests pressed graphite and coated nine LEU-235 fuel items. The Pressurizer used 24,000 FE and the Assembler used 6,000 FE. A separate test checked that overlapping ingredients still match each required slot. A silent, hidden client opened the Assembler with its four input slots and found all 27 recipes plus the Pressurizer’s pyrolytic carbon entry in JEI.
- Pebble-bed reactors have their own controller, chamber, and fuel port. The 27 legacy TRISO recipes retain their burn time, heat, efficiency, criticality, intrinsic flux, and self-priming values. Neighboring chambers with the same fuel share flux and criticality. The shell uses the shared graphite and beryllium moderation and neutron sources.
- All 16 gas cooler and port variants have independent Forge tanks, configurable cooling rates, and the legacy default placement rules. Each cooler turns 1 mB of cold gas into 2 mB of hot gas. Dry or blocked coolers stop conducting heat. The idle reactor can use separate emergency coolant vents. The turbine accepts every hot gas and produces 2 mB of the matching exhaust per mB of input.
- Headless tests sent the reactor’s hot gas to a turbine, verified mixed oxygen and nitrogen circuits with exact amounts through reassembly, and confirmed that a dry, detached chamber overheats before the casing. A silent, hidden client opened the controller, received both gas tank levels and a custom cooling rate from the server, and found all TRISO, cooler, and hot gas recipes in JEI.
- Condensers convert 16 mB of exhaust steam or 32 mB of low-quality steam into 1 mB of condensate. The original copper, hard-carbon, and thermoconducting tubes have separately configurable faces. Use the Multitool to cycle the clicked face, or sneak to configure the opposite face. Connected tubes form independent steam circuits. A shell inlet accepts a fluid at or below 300 K, and the shell keeps its coolant during operation.
- Headless tests verified the exact volumes from two independent circuits, dry-shell and full-output stalls, outward capability access and simulation, and restoring buffers after breaking and rebuilding the shell. A silent, hidden multiplayer client confirmed the shell gauge, circuit count, and both JEI recipes. The native focus observer recorded no visible windows or focus changes.
- Heat exchangers share the condenser's casing, ports, tube controls, and sided Forge fluid handlers. Standalone baffles and closed tube faces direct shell flow. Each tube circuit transfers heat only along shortest paths that meet the shell flow. Tube material, flow direction, temperature, and free space in both outputs limit transfer.
- The heat exchanger datapack contains all 70 legacy recipes. The catalog includes six water and steam recipes, recovery for 16 hot gases and their exhaust, and cooling for 32 NaK fluids. The condenser and exchanger controllers can be crafted into one another. The molten-salt reactor remains open.
- Headless tests reclaimed exactly 256 mB of oxygen from 1,024 mB of turbine exhaust while converting 64 mB of water into 256 mB of high-pressure steam. Other tests proved that a baffle can redirect flow across an otherwise bypassed tube and that a full shell output stalls both sides through disassembly and reassembly. A silent, hidden client displayed both shell tanks and all 70 recipes in JEI, including a server-selected alternate steam output and a custom gas heat multiplier.
- The Salt Mixer has the legacy crafting pattern and all 125 recipes, including 31 NaK variants and 49 fluoride mixtures. Two independently configurable input tanks accept either ingredient order. Filling a matching tank keeps the other input free for the second ingredient. The product tank supports hold, void excess, and void. The Electrolyzer shares the same processing code and retains its five tanks and existing save format.
- The Melter now supports both legacy hydroxide compounds and the available molten metal, mineral, and isotope inputs used by the mixer. Headless tests finished a 12,000 FE NaK batch after clearing a full output and reloading partial progress. A separate headless line melted one sodium hydroxide and two potassium hydroxide items, electrolyzed the fluids, and made 144 mB of NaK. Two linked mixers also formed FLiBe and prepared exactly 72 mB of LEU-235 fuel salt. A silent hidden client displayed all three tanks, synced a side change for only the second input, and listed all 125 Salt Mixer and 64 Electrolyzer recipes in JEI.
- The Chemical Reactor has the legacy crafting pattern and all 120 recipes, including 49 isotope and fuel fluorination recipes. Its two interchangeable input tanks and two ordered outlets each have side settings. Each outlet has its own overflow mode. The Melter supplies molten fuel to the Chemical Reactor, which makes the fuel fluoride used by the Salt Mixer. Two legacy recipes requested an unregistered carbon fluid. They use NuclearCraft’s existing molten coal. Headless GameTests loaded all 120 recipes, held a batch behind a full second outlet, and resumed it from saved progress without losing reactants. A separate test melted a fuel pellet, ran the Chemical Reactor, and used its output in the Salt Mixer to produce 72 mB of LEU-235 fuel salt. A hidden multiplayer client synced all four tanks and an independent second-outlet side change. JEI displayed both recipe outputs and loaded the full catalog. - Remaining work includes the molten-salt reactor, the Enricher that supplies fluorite water and other chemical ingredients, buffer migration when changing a heat exchanger or condenser circuit’s anchor inlet, condenser and exchanger fluid rendering inside glass shells, cold-gas chemical production, per-chamber and per-cooler filter menus, configurable placement rule overrides, automatic discovery of external plate recipes for the Pressurizer, neutron source managers, the polonium production path, the Extractor that produces the input for Soulless Sandstone, the Electrolyzer's advanced ingredients, extra coolers and cryogenic fluids, the rest of the Melter and reactor catalogs, reactor fuel decay effects, upgrades and per-slot automation modes, legacy port filters, turbine animated rendering, radiation, Wasteland generation, the contact and fire behavior of placed chemical fluids, and the other Overhauled systems.

## Verified with Auram mod JARs

A disposable headless Forge 47.4.10 server ran the reobfuscated NuclearCraft JAR with the exact Ender IO and Mekanism JARs from Auram. Each Melter received one ice and 4,000 FE. Server block data confirmed both destinations held exactly 1,000 mB of water and both Melter output tanks were empty.

- Melter → Ender IO Fluid Conduit → Ender IO Fluid Tank
- Melter → Mekanism Basic Mechanical Pipe in pull mode → Ender IO Fluid Tank

The same packaged reactor ran with each mod's pipes at both vents. Each setup emptied a 1,000 mB source tank, delivered 4,000 mB of high-pressure steam to its destination, and produced one depleted fuel item. The two devices were Ender IO Fluid Conduits and Mekanism Basic Mechanical Pipes, using Ender IO 8.2.17-beta and Mekanism 10.4.16.80. A full restart of the same test world preserved the steam volumes and depleted fuel in both setups.

Two packaged reactor-to-turbine chains used Ender IO conduits or Mekanism transmitters for each transfer. Each delivered one fuel item from a barrel, drew 1,000 mB of water from an Ender IO tank, collected 16,000 mB of turbine exhaust in another tank, and returned one depleted fuel item to an output barrel. The Mekanism Basic Universal Cable powered a Melter that produced 1,000 mB of water from ice. With both turbines starting as soon as steam arrived and both Melters paused, the Ender IO Energy Conduit delivered 2,441 FE and the Mekanism cable delivered 18,891 FE. The ice recipe requires 4,000 FE.

With the turbines enabled after each inlet accumulated exactly 4,000 mB of high-pressure steam, both networks delivered 5,051 FE to their paused Melters. After the Melters were enabled, each produced 1,000 mB of water from its ice.

In a separate headless test with Auram's installed KubeJS JAR, a server script removed the default Melter ice recipe and added a custom recipe with `event.custom`. The Melter produced the scripted 750 mB of water in-world.

With Auram's Almost Unified JAR and config alongside Mekanism, a furnace smelted one NuclearCraft copper dust into one vanilla copper ingot, matching the pack's priority for copper.

With separate north input and south output faces, Auram's Ender IO Item Conduits and Mekanism Basic Logistical Transporters each supplied one coal to a Manufactory and three copper plus one tin to an Alloy Furnace. Each network emptied its source barrels and collected one coal dust and four bronze ingots in the corresponding destination barrels.

For the Separator, Ender IO supplied ten Mekanism uranium ingots and Mekanism supplied ten NuclearCraft uranium dust. Both source barrels emptied and each destination collected exactly nine uranium-238 ingots and one uranium-235 ingot. A full restart preserved those counts. The server then confirmed the test chunk was unloaded before it was loaded again, and both source and destination inventories still matched.

The Infuser accepted 1,000 mB of Mekanism oxygen through an Ender IO Fluid Conduit and 1,000 mB of NuclearCraft oxygen through a Mekanism Basic Mechanical Pipe. Ender IO Item Conduits and Mekanism Basic Logistical Transporters supplied an LEU-235 pellet and extracted the oxide from each machine. Both networks emptied their source tanks and barrels and collected one LEU-235 oxide in each destination barrel.

Each started with 750 mB of buffered oxygen and delivered enough for one LEU-235 oxide. The source tanks and barrels emptied, and each destination barrel received exactly one oxide. A full server restart and a confirmed chunk unload and reload preserved the tank contents and item counts in both chains.

Compact Cobblestone Generators with the base power setting changed to 2.5 FE/t used a 400 FE buffer to produce exactly 20 cobblestone each, which the item conduit or transporter collected. The pebble cooler metadata order is oxygen, hydrogen, helium, nitrogen, fluorine, methane, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, ethene, ethyne, fluoromethane, ammonia, diborane, sulfur dioxide, sulfur trioxide, and sulfur hexafluoride. Replace spaces with underscores in registry IDs. The original `pebble_fission_controller`, `pebble_fission_chamber`, and `fission_chamber_port` names are retained. The datapack recipe type `nuclearcraft:pebble_fission` uses the same fuel fields as `nuclearcraft:solid_fission`.

The legacy `fission_cooler_cooling_rate` array maps to the `cooling_rate` section of `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-pebble-fission.toml`, using the gas names as keys. Its `coolant_heat_per_mb`, `intrinsic_flux_efficiency`, and `emergency_cooling_multiplier` replace `fission_cooler_coolant_heat_per_mb`, `fission_chamber_intrinsic_flux_efficiency`, and `fission_emergency_cooling_multiplier`. Gas inputs use the corresponding `forge:<gas>` fluid tag. Hot gas and turbine exhaust use the `<gas>_hot` and `<gas>_exhaust` suffixes.

Legacy `nuclearcraft:material_block` metadata 0 and 1 maps to `nuclearcraft:molybdenum_block` and `nuclearcraft:copper_oxide_block`. Pressurizer and Assembler recipes use `nuclearcraft:pressurizer` and `nuclearcraft:assembler` with a single `result`. The Assembler takes four counted ingredients and matches them in any order.

Legacy `fertile_isotope` variants 0 through 6 map in order to `uranium_238_block`, `neptunium_237_block`, `plutonium_242_block`, `americium_243_block`, `curium_246_block`, `berkelium_247_block`, and `californium_252_block` in the `nuclearcraft` namespace.

The datapack recipe types include `nuclearcraft:salt_mixer`, `nuclearcraft:chemical_reactor`, `nuclearcraft:manufactory`, `nuclearcraft:separator`, `nuclearcraft:alloy_furnace`, `nuclearcraft:decay_hastener`, `nuclearcraft:infuser`, `nuclearcraft:electrolyzer`, `nuclearcraft:melter`, `nuclearcraft:solid_fission`, `nuclearcraft:turbine`, `nuclearcraft:condenser`, and `nuclearcraft:heat_exchanger`. Item processor recipes take counted Forge item ingredients. The Separator uses a `results` array in output slot order. Manufactory, Alloy Furnace, Decay Hastener, and Infuser recipes use a single `result` object. Alloy Furnace inputs are interchangeable. The Infuser and Electrolyzer have a `fluid_input` object with a `fluid` ID or a `tag` and an `amount` in mB. The Electrolyzer's `results` array contains up to four `fluid` and `amount` objects in outlet tank order. Salt Mixer and Chemical Reactor recipes use two counted, interchangeable `fluid_inputs`. The Salt Mixer has one fluid `result`. The Chemical Reactor takes a `results` array of one or two fluids in outlet order. Turbine, condenser, and heat exchanger recipes accept fluid tags. Condenser and heat exchanger recipes use a fluid `input`, a fluid `output`, and heat or cooling and temperature values. Legacy world data still requires an explicit conversion.

The legacy heat exchanger size limits, tube conductivity and retention arrays, gas and NaK heat multipliers, logarithmic temperature mean, and alternate steam outputs map to `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-heat-exchangers.toml`. The gas recipes read the current pebble-bed coolant heat setting. `nak_coolant_heat_per_mb` corresponds to the legacy fission heater coolant heat value. Tube face settings live in the Forge block states `closed`, `open`, `closed_baffle`, and `open_baffle`. The condenser retains its existing Forge controller ID and saved coolant format.


Legacy `ore`, `ingot` and `ingot2`, `dust` and `dust2`, and `ingot_block` and `ingot_block2` variants use per-material IDs such as `nuclearcraft:boron_ore`, `nuclearcraft:zirconia_ingot`, `nuclearcraft:zirconia_dust`, and `nuclearcraft:lithium_block`. The metadata index follows each corresponding legacy enum. Alloy variants use the same `<material>_ingot` pattern.

Ore generation uses Forge biome modifiers and configured and placed features. The default modifiers target the Overworld biome tag with the legacy height bands and attempts per chunk. A pack can override `data/nuclearcraft/forge/biome_modifier/ore_<material>.json` with `{"type":"forge:none"}` to disable an ore, or replace the configured and placed features to control its shape, height, and frequency.

Collector settings moved to `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-collectors.toml`. Use `defaultconfigs/nuclearcraft-collectors.toml` as a template for new worlds. The three entries of legacy `processor_passive_rate` map to `collectors.cobblestone_rate`, `collectors.water_rate`, and `collectors.nitrogen_rate`. The collector-specific equivalents of `machine_update_rate`, `passive_push`, and `cobble_gen_power` are `collectors.update_interval`, `collectors.auto_output`, and `collectors.cobblestone_power`. Setting a family's production rate to zero stops generation while retaining its block IDs. The three legacy `register_passive` entries still need an explicit equivalent for registry availability.

The three entries of legacy `fission_source_efficiency` map to the `neutron_sources` section in `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-fission.toml`. Its settings are `radium_beryllium_efficiency`, `polonium_beryllium_efficiency`, and `californium_efficiency`, with defaults of 0.9, 0.95, and 1 respectively.

In the same file, `neutrons.reach` and `neutrons.intrinsic_flux_efficiency` replace legacy `fission_neutron_reach` and `fission_cell_intrinsic_flux_efficiency`. The entries of `fission_moderator_flux_factor` and `fission_moderator_efficiency` map to `flux` and `efficiency` in `moderators.graphite`, `moderators.beryllium`, and `moderators.heavy_water`. The entries of `fission_reflector_reflectivity` and `fission_reflector_efficiency` map to `reflectivity` and `efficiency` in `reflectors.beryllium_carbon` and `reflectors.lead_steel`.

The Decay Generator uses `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-generators.toml`. Its `decay_generator.update_interval` maps to the legacy generator’s use of `machine_update_rate`. The original `decay_lifetime` and `decay_power` arrays map to the `lifetime` and `power` values under the nine named material sections in their original order. Recipes of type `nuclearcraft:decay_generator` take a Forge item `ingredient` for a block and a `result` block ID. An optional `result_tag` selects an available block ahead of the fixed result. Specify a server-configured `family` or both `lifetime` in ticks and `power` in FE per tick.

Legacy Fuel Reprocessor recipes can be expressed as `nuclearcraft:fuel_reprocessor` datapack recipes. Use a counted ingredient and a `results` array with eight entries in row-major slot order. Use `null` for an inactive output, and `count`, `min_count`, and `chance_percent` for the same independent rolls as the Rock Crusher. The 1.12.2 IC2 depleted fuel recipes still need mappings for a compatible modern IC2 variant.

Legacy `rock_crusher_alternate` maps to `rock_crusher.alternate_outputs` in `world/serverconfig/nuclearcraft-processors.toml`. The `nuclearcraft:rock_crusher` recipe type uses `results` in slot order and can use `alternate_results` with the same format. Each result specifies an item and maximum `count`, with optional `min_count` and `chance_percent` fields. The chance applies independently to each possible item above the minimum.
